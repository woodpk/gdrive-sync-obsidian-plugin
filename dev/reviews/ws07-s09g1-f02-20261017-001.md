## F02-01 — HIGH — Deauthorization does not stop an active synchronization run before changing identity

**Source:** `src/product/runtime.ts`, `deauthorize()` (approximately lines 645–661), `disposeProduct()` (664–679); `src/product/product-controller-base.ts`, running execution lifecycle.

**Observed control flow:** Deauthorization increments `runtimeGeneration` and stops the scheduler, then **awaits** an asynchronous settings persistence operation changing the remote pairing/namespace. Only after persistence does it clear OAuth sessions and call `disposeProduct()`, which invokes `controller.beginRuntimeDisposal()`. Stopping the scheduler does not synchronously cancel/stop a currently executing controller run. During the awaited settings save, that older controller remains live and retains its captured remote Drive boundary/state authority. The completed persistent identity transition can therefore precede cessation of old-pairing remote and local mutations. A running operation may also continue until disposal is requested.

**Contract:** AUTH-011/AUTH-012, STATE-008–STATE-010, PLAN-005/PLAN-009, LIFE-002, and the system's safe deauthorization/pairing isolation semantics require retired identity to cease authorizing operations and durable state to reflect only operations in the correct pairing epoch.

**Required correction:** Retire and quiesce or cancel the active old controller and invalidate old OAuth operations at the start of deauthorization, before any awaited durable unpairing transition; preserve safe atomic-operation and durable-commit ordering. Ensure no old controller can begin/commit further pairing-authoritative operations after deauthorization is accepted. Continue to preserve local/shared vault content.

**Objective acceptance:** A deterministic interleaving in which an active sync is suspended at an asynchronous operation while deauthorization begins must prove old-pairing mutations/completion cannot proceed into the new/unpaired epoch; the settings change and subsequent re-pair remain isolated.

## F02-02 — MODERATE — Runtime initialization can publish an obsolete runtime after a competing initializer starts

**Source:** `src/product/runtime.ts`, `initialize()` (79–340), notably `await this.disposeProduct(false)` and subsequent writes to shared `this.attentionPersistence`, `this.boundary`, `this.local`, `this.controller`, and `this.scheduler`.

**Observed control flow:** Every `initialize()` increments `runtimeGeneration`, but `disposeProduct(false)` is awaited before the first generation check. A later initializer can start while an earlier initializer awaits disposal. Generation checks protect several awaits, but construction and publication of the OAuth boundary and final controller/scheduler are not serialized as one atomic owner transition; a superseding initializer can retire an older one between its last checked await and subsequent publication of shared fields. A retired initializer can then publish/overwrite its controller/scheduler or other shared runtime reference.

**Contract:** SYNC-008/SYNC-009, STATE-008, AUTH-011/AUTH-012 and the runtime-authority isolation boundary prohibit competing active writers or stale pairing runtime publication.

**Required correction:** Serialize initializer ownership or ensure a generation-checked guarded publication and cleanup protocol at every asynchronous boundary, with only the newest epoch allowed to own shared runtime fields. Disposal of obsolete work must never clear a successor's references.

**Objective acceptance:** Exercise overlapping initializations with controlled await boundaries and demonstrate only newest-epoch runtime ownership and exactly one active scheduler/controller.
